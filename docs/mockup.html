Teaching tips for this week (written before term)

Round 6 is the first instrument choice. Expect most mayors to set the tax below $200. Point at the tile grid: chosen tax vs the city's marginal damage. Ask why nobody sets $200.

Business notes will complain. Read one aloud. Ask who bears the tax: the business, its customers, or the borough.

Cap vs tax gives the same outcome this week because costs are known. Round 7 breaks that.

Worked example to project · round 1

What students see: two sliders, three numbers. No policy, no chart.

Walk through Ridgeline Cement (type 3). Output 100, containment 0: profit $3,000, 20 t of ghosts. Move containment to 50%: profit falls to $2,250, ghosts to 10 t. Nobody pays you for those 10 t, so a profit-maximizing business picks 0%. Say that out loud. That is the externality.

Then: 45 businesses all pick 0%. 900 t go into the air. Monday the Ether reads 0.31. Ask what stops it rising.

Worked example to project · round 2

What changes: the borough page shows the city marginal damage line at $200 over the borough MAC.

Walk through Coalbrook: containment target 0% costs nothing and does nothing. Target 46% is where borough MAC meets $200. Shade the triangle between them. That is the deadweight loss of free-riding, per borough, per week.

Ask: if Coalbrook alone hits 46% and everyone else stays at 0%, does Coalbrook come out ahead? (No. Their damages barely move.)

Worked example to project · round 3

Decision: discount rate, 2% to 7%, locked.

Show: the same 12-round haunting path discounted at 2% and at 7%. The 7% borough values round 12 hauntings at about half of what the 2% borough does, so it will contain less now and look better on the leaderboard early. Ask who they think wins by round 12.
