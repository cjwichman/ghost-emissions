Ghost Emissions — demo

A semester-long climate economics game for an undergraduate class. This page runs the real model against nine bot boroughs. No login, no database, nothing written anywhere. Reload to start over.

What you are looking at

You run a business in one of nine boroughs in a city. You want to be profitable, but economic activity emits ghosts, which cause slime damage to everyone in the city, and containing them is costly. Each borough's mayor, rotating among the business owners, can set policies to contain ghosts and generate benefits (fewer ghosts released, less slime damage), but has to balance those benefits against the cost of containing them.

Ghosts stand in for greenhouse gases, the Ether for global temperature, slime damage for climate damages. Ghosts accumulate, so the Ether keeps rising until the city releases fewer of them.

Two decisions a week, both due Sunday night. Each student sets output and containment for their own business. A rotating mayor sets one policy for the borough, which binds that borough's businesses the following week. Results resolve Monday and get projected in class. Twelve scored rounds plus a practice week, one per teaching week.

Underneath is a stock pollutant with linear marginal abatement costs, quadratic damages and concave business revenue. A price of $200 a ton is the city optimum, calibrated so that the price and the marginal damage are a fixed point rather than an assumption. The rounds add one lever at a time: a discount rate, a damage revision, a vote on the price, tax against cap against standard, a cost shock, revenue recycling, R&D, adaptation and a tipping point, geoengineering, and a treaty settled by an in-class vote.

Rank is never graded. Credit comes from submitting, capped so that two missed weeks cost a student nothing.

Run the rounds below with the buttons. Take a borough yourself to try a tool against the bots. Nothing here touches the live class: real play needs accounts and a class code.
